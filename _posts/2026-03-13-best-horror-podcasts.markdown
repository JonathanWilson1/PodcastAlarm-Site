These are the 15 best horror podcasts to listen to in 2026: scripted audio drama, horror anthologies, creepypasta narration and dark folklore, all still releasing episodes. Every entry comes with the one episode to start on, so you can try a show tonight instead of scrolling a back catalogue.</p>

<h2>What's new for 2026</h2>
<p>This year's refresh added five shows and sharpened the focus. <strong>Malevolent</strong> and <strong>The Silt Verses</strong> are now essential listening for anyone who came to the genre through The Magnus Archives, and both finally get the placement they deserve. <strong>The Magnus Archives</strong> joins its own sequel so newcomers have an entry point. We've also moved the real-encounter shows — Spooked, Uncanny, Radio Rental, Astonishing Legends — over to the <a class="text-info" href="/blog/best-paranormal-podcasts">paranormal list</a> where they belong, so this page is purely scary fiction and scary stories.</p>

<h2>Best horror fiction and audio drama podcasts</h2>
<p>Scripted, full-cast horror with sound design built to be heard in the dark. If you want the closest thing audio has to a horror film, start here.</p>

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/us/search?term=The+Magnus+Protocol">1. The Magnus Protocol</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-horror-podcasts/magnus-protocol-horror.jpg" alt="The Magnus Protocol podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>The follow-up to The Magnus Archives, written by Jonathan Sims and Alexander J Newall. Set in a government office that catalogues impossible incidents, it builds slow cosmic dread out of bureaucratic tedium — mundane workplace chat interrupted by genuinely disturbing case files. You don't need the original to get into it.</p>
<p><strong>Start with:</strong> episode 1, "First Shift" — it establishes the format and the first case is a nasty one.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/us/search?term=The+Magnus+Archives+Rusty+Quill">2. The Magnus Archives</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-horror-podcasts/magnus-archives-horror.jpg" alt="The Magnus Archives podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>The show that rewrote the rules for horror podcasting. An archivist reads witness statements from a paranormal institute — one self-contained horror story per episode — until the statements start connecting into something much larger. 200 episodes, a properly satisfying ending, and the best structural payoff in the genre.</p>
<p><strong>Start with:</strong> episode 1, "Anglerfish". It's five minutes long and still the best advert the show has.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/us/search?term=Malevolent+Harlan+Guthrie">3. Malevolent</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-horror-podcasts/malevolent-horror.jpg" alt="Malevolent podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>A private eye in 1930s Arkham wakes up blind, with something else living behind his eyes. Harlan Guthrie writes, performs and produces almost the whole thing single-handedly, and the two-hander between Arthur and the entity is one of the best character relationships in audio fiction. Lovecraftian, but with actual heart.</p>
<p><strong>Start with:</strong> part 1 — it's serialised, so there's no jumping in halfway.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/us/search?term=The+Silt+Verses">4. The Silt Verses</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-horror-podcasts/silt-verses-horror.jpg" alt="The Silt Verses podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>Two worshippers walk a river in a country where gods are real, licensed and hungry. Folk horror by way of corporate bureaucracy, with writing so good it occasionally stops being horror and just becomes literature. It won a stack of awards and finished its story properly rather than dragging it out.</p>
<p><strong>Start with:</strong> chapter 1, "The Nesting Grounds" — the opening five minutes tell you whether this is for you.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/us/search?term=Old+Gods+of+Appalachia">5. Old Gods of Appalachia</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-horror-podcasts/old-gods-appalachia-horror.jpg" alt="Old Gods of Appalachia podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>Eldritch horror set in an alternate Appalachia where ancient things sleep beneath the hills and the coal mines go deeper than they should. Literary and atmospheric — more Southern Gothic than shock horror — and narrated in a voice that could read a shopping list and still unsettle you. Season six launched in January 2026.</p>
<p><strong>Start with:</strong> "Build Mama a Coffin, Part 1" — the show's calling card.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/us/search?term=The+White+Vault+Fool+and+Scholar">6. The White Vault</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-horror-podcasts/white-vault-horror.jpg" alt="The White Vault podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>A repair team is sent to a remote Arctic research outpost and finds something under the ice. Found-footage horror done as audio, with a huge international cast and sound design that makes the cold feel like a character. Later seasons jump to new locations, so the scale keeps growing.</p>
<p><strong>Start with:</strong> season 1, episode 1 — the Svalbard season is still the best one.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/us/search?term=Video+Palace+Shudder">7. Video Palace</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-horror-podcasts/video-palace-horror.jpg" alt="Video Palace podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>A film collector finds a mysterious white VHS tape that makes him speak an unknown language in his sleep. Shudder's audio drama nails the analogue-horror aesthetic without leaning on nostalgia, and at ten episodes it's the tightest binge on this list.</p>
<p><strong>Start with:</strong> episode 1 — it's a single ten-part story.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/us/search?term=Limetown+Two+Up">8. Limetown</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-horror-podcasts/limetown-horror.jpg" alt="Limetown podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>Three hundred people vanish overnight from a research town in Tennessee, and a journalist spends a decade trying to find out why. Presented as investigative radio, which makes the horror land as dread rather than scares. One of the originals of the format and still one of the most imitated.</p>
<p><strong>Start with:</strong> season 1, episode 1, "What We Know" — the interview at the end is the hook.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/us/search?term=The+Black+Tapes+Pacific+Northwest+Stories">9. The Black Tapes</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-horror-podcasts/black-tapes-horror.jpg" alt="The Black Tapes podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>A journalist profiles a paranormal debunker and ends up working through his shelf of cases he couldn't explain. The sceptic-versus-believer format keeps the tension honest, and the demonology thread that builds across the series is properly creepy. A serial-style classic of the genre.</p>
<p><strong>Start with:</strong> episode 1, "A Unicorn Hunter".</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/us/search?term=Unwell+a+Midwestern+Gothic+Mystery">10. Unwell, a Midwestern Gothic Mystery</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-horror-podcasts/unwell-horror.jpg" alt="Unwell a Midwestern Gothic Mystery podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>A woman returns to her small Ohio hometown to care for her estranged mother and finds the boarding house — and the town — keeping secrets. Quieter and more character-driven than most horror fiction here, which makes the moments it does turn nasty land much harder.</p>
<p><strong>Start with:</strong> episode 1, "Welcome to Mt. Absalom, Ohio".</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/us/search?term=We%27re+Alive+Zombie+Audio+Drama">11. We're Alive</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-horror-podcasts/were-alive-horror.jpg" alt="We're Alive podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>A full-cast zombie audio drama with production values that rival television. The current series, We're Alive: Descendants, returned in late 2025 and keeps expanding a post-apocalyptic Los Angeles. Nearly 200 episodes across the various series, with the acting and sound design consistently excellent.</p>
<p><strong>Start with:</strong> the original series, chapter 1 — Descendants assumes you've heard it.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h2>Best horror anthology and scary story podcasts</h2>
<p>One story per episode, no continuity homework. These are the best scary podcasts for dipping in and out — or for when you want a fright that's over in twenty minutes.</p>

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/us/search?term=The+NoSleep+Podcast">12. The NoSleep Podcast</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-horror-podcasts/nosleep-podcast-horror.jpg" alt="The NoSleep Podcast podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>Now in its 24th season, the longest-running horror anthology in podcasting. Every episode collects several original stories performed by a rotating voice cast with full sound design. The quality varies story to story, but after a decade of weekly episodes the hit rate is remarkably high.</p>
<p><strong>Start with:</strong> any season premiere — they're double-length and the production budget shows.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/us/search?term=Knifepoint+Horror+Soren+Narnia">13. Knifepoint Horror</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-horror-podcasts/knifepoint-horror-podcast.jpg" alt="Knifepoint Horror podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>Soren Narnia writes and narrates stripped-back horror with no music and no sound effects — just one voice telling you something terrible happened. The minimalism is exactly why it works. New episodes dropped as recently as 2026.</p>
<p><strong>Start with:</strong> "Staircase" — the most recommended episode in the show's history, and deservedly so.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/us/search?term=Creepy+Jon+Grilz">14. Creepy</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-horror-podcasts/creepy-podcast-horror.jpg" alt="Creepy podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>Jon Grilz narrates creepypasta and original short horror in a low, unhurried voice that does most of the work. If you grew up reading horror threads at 2am, this is that experience with better pacing and a professional read. Hundreds of episodes, with a new one most weeks.</p>
<p><strong>Start with:</strong> "The Russian Sleep Experiment" — the classic, done properly.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/us/search?term=Scared+to+Death+Dan+Cummins">15. Scared to Death</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-horror-podcasts/scared-to-death-horror.jpg" alt="Scared to Death podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>Comedian Dan Cummins and his wife Lynze alternate between a scary story and listener-submitted real experiences. The comedy framing sounds like it should undercut the horror and somehow does the opposite — you relax, then it gets you. A good gateway if full audio drama feels like a commitment.</p>
<p><strong>Start with:</strong> any episode; the two-story format means there's no running order to learn.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h2>And one for dark folklore</h2>

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/us/search?term=Lore+Aaron+Mahnke">Lore</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-horror-podcasts/lore-podcast-horror.jpg" alt="Lore podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>Aaron Mahnke digs into the dark folklore, legends and real historical events behind our fears. Each episode is a self-contained 30-minute deep dive — the kind that makes you Google "wait, did that actually happen?" afterwards. Over 500 million downloads and nearly 300 episodes without losing a step. It sits on this list and our paranormal one, because it genuinely belongs on both.</p>
<p><strong>Start with:</strong> episode 1, "They Made a Tonic" — vampires, tuberculosis and 19th-century New England.</p>

<h2>Horror podcast FAQs</h2>

<h3>What is the scariest podcast to listen to?</h3>
<p>For pure dread, <strong>Knifepoint Horror</strong> — one unaccompanied voice, no music to hide behind. For sustained scares across a long series, <strong>The Magnus Archives</strong>. If you want something that actually makes you check the locks, listen to either on headphones with the lights off.</p>

<h3>What's the best horror podcast for beginners?</h3>
<p><strong>Lore</strong> or <strong>The NoSleep Podcast</strong>. Both are self-contained episode to episode, so you can sample one and stop without unfinished business. Save the serialised audio dramas for once you know you like the format.</p>

<h3>Are these horror podcasts free?</h3>
<p>Yes — every show here is free on Apple Podcasts and other major apps. Several also run paid feeds with bonus episodes and ad-free listening, but the main run is free.</p>
