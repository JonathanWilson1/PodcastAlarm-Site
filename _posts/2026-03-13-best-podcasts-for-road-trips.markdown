<p><em>Last updated July 2026.</em></p>

<p>The best road trip podcasts aren't just background noise — they're the ones that make you sit in the car park for ten minutes because you need to hear what happens next. Here are the shows built for long drives: serialised stories, deep dives, and conversations so good you'll wish the journey was longer. We've grouped them so you can jump straight to what fits your trip — <a class="text-info" href="#long-drives">epic long-drive listens</a>, <a class="text-info" href="#funny">funny podcasts for the car</a>, <a class="text-info" href="#family">family road trips with kids</a>, or <a class="text-info" href="#solo">a solo drive</a>.</p>

{% include callToActionRow.html %}

<h3>What's new for summer 2026</h3>
<p>This is peak road-trip season, so we've expanded the guide beyond the core top 10. There are now dedicated picks for <strong>long drives</strong> (multi-hour epics like Hardcore History), <strong>funny podcasts</strong> to keep the car laughing, and <strong>family road trips</strong> with kid-friendly shows the whole car can enjoy. Every show below is still actively releasing episodes.</p>

<br>

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/search?term=S-Town">1. S-Town</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-podcasts-for-road-trips/s-town.jpg" alt="S-Town podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>Brian Reed investigates a tip from a man named John B. McLemore in small-town Alabama, and what unfolds is one of the most extraordinary stories ever told in audio. All seven episodes dropped at once, which means you can burn through the whole thing in a single long drive. It's gripping, heartbreaking, and completely true.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/search?term=Casefile+True+Crime">2. Casefile True Crime</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-podcasts-for-road-trips/casefile-true-crime.jpg" alt="Casefile True Crime podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>An anonymous Australian narrator walks you through real criminal cases with meticulous research and zero sensationalism. Some cases span multiple episodes, others fit into a single hour — either way, you won't notice the miles ticking by. Nearly 400 episodes deep, so you'll never run out.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/search?term=Radiolab">3. Radiolab</a></h3>
<p>Lulu Miller and Latif Nasser explore science, philosophy, and the strange corners of human experience with some of the most creative sound design in podcasting. Episodes run 30-60 minutes and cover everything from the nature of colour to the ethics of gene editing. Perfect for sparking a conversation with whoever's in the passenger seat.</p>

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/search?term=Heavyweight">4. Heavyweight</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-podcasts-for-road-trips/heavyweight.jpg" alt="Heavyweight podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>Jonathan Goldstein helps real people resolve unfinished business from their past — a stolen high school painting, a falling out between brothers, a regret that's been eating at someone for decades. Each episode is funny, tender, and surprisingly moving. The kind of show that makes everyone in the car go quiet at the end.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/search?term=Revisionist+History">5. Revisionist History</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-podcasts-for-road-trips/revisionist-history.jpg" alt="Revisionist History podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>Malcolm Gladwell re-examines things we thought we understood: forgotten events, misunderstood people, overlooked ideas. Episodes run about 40 minutes and are self-contained, so you can dip in anywhere. His recent multi-part series on the 1936 Olympics is worth the drive alone.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/search?term=99%25+Invisible">6. 99% Invisible</a></h3>
<p>Roman Mars explains the hidden design decisions behind everyday things — why hospital gowns look the way they do, how a font ended up on every street sign, why revolving doors exist. Episodes are around 30 minutes, tightly produced, and endlessly interesting. Stack a few and the hours disappear.</p>

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/search?term=Armchair+Expert">7. Armchair Expert</a></h3>
<p>Dax Shepard and Monica Padman have long, honest conversations with actors, scientists, and authors. Episodes run nearly two hours, which sounds like a lot until you're three hours into a drive and realise you've listened to two back-to-back without noticing. The chemistry between the hosts makes everything feel relaxed and real.</p>

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/search?term=No+Such+Thing+As+A+Fish">8. No Such Thing As A Fish</a></h3>
<p>The researchers behind QI share the most bizarre facts they've found each week. Episodes are about 50 minutes of four funny people trying to one-up each other with increasingly absurd trivia. It's light, it's quick, and you can rattle through five or six episodes on a decent road trip.</p>

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/search?term=Stuff+You+Should+Know">9. Stuff You Should Know</a></h3>
<p>Josh Clark and Chuck Bryant have been explaining how things work since 2008, and they've covered nearly everything — from how landfills work to the history of LSD to the science behind quicksand. Episodes run about an hour, the tone is like two mates chatting, and with over 2,700 episodes you'll never hit the end of the back catalogue.</p>

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/search?term=The+Rest+Is+History">10. The Rest Is History</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-podcasts-for-road-trips/the-rest-is-history.jpg" alt="The Rest Is History podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>Historians Tom Holland and Dominic Sandbrook make history genuinely entertaining. Their multi-part series on the fall of the Roman Empire, the French Revolution, and the Titanic are perfect for eating up long stretches of motorway. Over 20 million monthly downloads for a reason — it's the most popular history podcast in the world.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h2 id="long-drives">Podcasts for long drives</h2>

<p>Some journeys need more than a 30-minute episode. For a genuinely long drive — think six hours of motorway or an overnight haul — you want shows with epic, hours-long episodes you can sink into without constantly reaching for your phone. These three are the heavyweight champions of long-distance listening.</p>

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/search?term=Dan+Carlin+Hardcore+History">Dan Carlin's Hardcore History</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-podcasts-for-road-trips/hardcore-history.jpg" alt="Dan Carlin's Hardcore History podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>Individual episodes routinely run four to six hours, and Dan Carlin narrates history with the intensity of a thriller. His "Blueprint for Armageddon" series on World War I is over 20 hours across six parts — enough to carry you across an entire country. If you want a single show that can fill a whole road trip, this is the one.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/search?term=Wind+of+Change+podcast">Wind of Change</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-podcasts-for-road-trips/wind-of-change.jpg" alt="Wind of Change podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>Journalist Patrick Radden Keefe chases a wild rumour: that the CIA secretly wrote the Scorpions' power ballad "Wind of Change" to help end the Cold War. It's a tight, bingeable investigative series you can finish in one long stretch, and the twists keep you gripped mile after mile.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/search?term=Lore+podcast">Lore</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-podcasts-for-road-trips/lore.jpg" alt="Lore podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>Aaron Mahnke digs into the true history behind folklore, superstition and the macabre — the real events that inspired our darkest legends. With over 250 episodes in the back catalogue, you can queue up a dozen and let the miles disappear. Best enjoyed on a night drive, if you dare.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h2 id="funny">Funny podcasts for the drive</h2>

<p>Nothing keeps a driver alert like laughing out loud. If you'd rather the car echo with jokes than true-crime tension, these comedy podcasts are made for the road — easy to dip into, no homework required, and genuinely funny with a passenger or on your own. For even more, see our full <a class="text-info" href="/blog/best-comedy-podcasts">best comedy podcasts</a> and <a class="text-info" href="/blog/best-british-comedy-podcasts">best British comedy podcasts</a> roundups.</p>

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/search?term=Conan+O%27Brien+Needs+a+Friend">Conan O'Brien Needs a Friend</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-podcasts-for-road-trips/conan-obrien.jpg" alt="Conan O'Brien Needs a Friend podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>Conan and his long-suffering assistant Sona interview famous friends, but the real joy is the loose, improvised riffing that spins off in every direction. Episodes run about an hour, land somewhere between an interview and a comedy sketch, and are perfect for laughing your way through traffic.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/search?term=SmartLess">SmartLess</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-podcasts-for-road-trips/smartless.jpg" alt="SmartLess podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>Jason Bateman, Sean Hayes and Will Arnett take turns surprising each other with a mystery guest, and the ribbing between the three of them is the whole point. It's warm, quick and endlessly quotable — the kind of show that makes a passenger snort with laughter and a solo driver grin at the windscreen.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/search?term=The+Adam+Buxton+Podcast">The Adam Buxton Podcast</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-podcasts-for-road-trips/adam-buxton.jpg" alt="The Adam Buxton Podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>Adam Buxton rambles, jingles and chats his way through conversations with musicians, comedians and writers, always with a gentle, oddball charm. His homemade theme tunes alone are worth the drive. A cosy, low-stakes listen that's ideal for a relaxed cross-country cruise.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h2 id="family">Family road trip podcasts (kid-friendly)</h2>

<p>A family road trip needs something everyone can enjoy — content that keeps kids entertained without driving the grown-ups round the bend. These shows are built for curious young minds but are genuinely fun for adults too, so nobody's reaching for the volume dial. For more, browse our full guide to the <a class="text-info" href="/blog/best-podcasts-for-kids">best podcasts for kids</a>.</p>

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/search?term=Wow+in+the+World">Wow in the World</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-podcasts-for-road-trips/wow-in-the-world.jpg" alt="Wow in the World podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>Guy Raz and Mindy Thomas take kids on a hilarious tour of the latest science and technology news, packed with silly songs and sound effects. It's high-energy enough to hold a five-year-old's attention and clever enough that parents actually learn something. A road trip staple for families.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/search?term=Brains+On">Brains On!</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-podcasts-for-road-trips/brains-on.jpg" alt="Brains On! podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>A science podcast for kids and families that answers big questions — why is the sky blue, how do animals see in the dark, what actually is a black hole. A different kid co-host joins each episode, and the explanations are clear enough for the whole car to follow.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h3><a class="text-info" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/search?term=But+Why+A+Podcast+for+Curious+Kids">But Why: A Podcast for Curious Kids</a></h3>
<img src="/images/blog/charts/best-podcasts-for-road-trips/but-why.jpg" alt="But Why: A Podcast for Curious Kids podcast artwork" width="100" style="border-radius:12px;float:left;margin:0 15px 15px 0;">
<p>Kids send in the questions and the show goes off to find answers from real experts. Episodes are short, calm and endlessly curious — a gentler option than the high-octane shows, and great for winding a car full of kids down on the back half of a long journey.</p>
<br style="clear:both;">

<h2 id="solo">Solo road trip podcasts</h2>

<p>Driving alone is where podcasts earn their keep. With no one to talk to, the right show becomes your co-pilot — and on a solo trip the priority is staying alert and engaged, so lean towards immersive stories and lively conversation over anything too soothing. The serialised picks up top are ideal: <strong>S-Town</strong> and <strong>Casefile</strong> pull you through with narrative momentum, while <strong>Armchair Expert</strong> and <strong>Dan Carlin's Hardcore History</strong> feel like good company for hours on end. Queue three or four before you set off so you're never fumbling with your phone at 70mph.
